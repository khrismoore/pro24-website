Helpful Answers

Before Your Exterior Project Begins

Every property and every project is different. These are useful starting points when you're deciding what to do next.

What should I do when I notice a roof leak?
Protect the area inside your home if you can do so safely, move belongings away from active water, and document what you see. Avoid climbing onto a wet, icy, steep, or storm-damaged roof. Water can travel along decking, rafters, or insulation before becoming visible indoors, so the ceiling stain may not be directly beneath the exterior opening. A professional inspection can help locate the entry point, assess the surrounding materials, and determine whether a focused repair or a broader solution makes sense.
When should I request an inspection after a storm?
If hail, high winds, falling branches, or wind-driven rain affected your neighborhood, it is reasonable to have the exterior checked even when the damage is not obvious from the ground. Missing or lifted shingles deserve prompt attention, as do dents in soft metal, displaced siding, damaged gutters, water intrusion, and debris impacts. Keep photos, weather dates, and any emergency invoices together. Pro24 can inspect the visible systems and explain the findings without asking you to diagnose the damage yourself.
Can roofing, siding, windows, and gutters be coordinated?
Yes. Exterior systems overlap physically and affect the order of work. Roof edges meet gutters; siding details meet windows; flashing and water-management components protect several assemblies at once. Coordinating connected work through one contractor can make sequencing and communication simpler. The right scope depends on the condition of each system, so start with the immediate concern and discuss the complete exterior before materials are ordered.
How can I prepare for the first conversation?
Share the property type, the concern you noticed, when it began, and any recent storm event. Photos from the ground or inside the building can be helpful, but they are not required. Mention prior repairs, known leak locations, access concerns, pets, tenants, or scheduling constraints. If an insurance claim may be involved, keep the claim information available. The first goal is simple: understand the condition, identify the next practical step, and give you room to ask questions.
